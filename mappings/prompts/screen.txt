# CONCEPTUAL BASIS

## Biodiversity: 
Biodiversity includes diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems.

## Essential Biodiversity Variables (EBVs): 
EBVs are a set of standardized biological measurements that help scientists study, report and manage changes in biodiversity across time, space, and biological level. 
It is possible that you observe different EBVs reported as measure of change in input text. 

## Forms of biodiversity changes
Changes in biodiversity may be be reported in one or more of these four broad categories. The categories are not fully exhaustive and may occur together in together. The text explainers provided are for example only. True range of each category may be broader and more illustrative.

1. Genetic change: genetic change usually means a change in the genetic diversity within a species or population; that is, changes in the variety of genes, alleles, or inherited traits among individuals. You may find different EBVs reporting change in input text

2. Species change: a change in the species-level composition of life in an area; simply, which species are present, how many there are, and how balanced they are relative to one another and different measures for it.  You may find different EBVs reporting change in input text 

3. Community change: a change in the composition or structure of a biological community over time. A community is all the different species living together in one place, such as plants, animals, fungi, and microbes in a forest, lake, or grassland.

4. Ecosystem change:  a change in the ecosystem itself, not just in one species, but in the whole system of living things, their physical environment, environmental settings, and the way they interact. Changes including, but not limited to, deterioration in structure, function, environment, integrity, extent, connectivity, degradation, fragmentation, or collapse risk.

# SCREENING STEPS

## Step 1. Biodiversity change component
Does the input text report at least one type of biodiversity change? If so, which type(s) are reported?

A list of forms of biodiversity changes is described earlier

## Step 2. Direction of change
Does the input text mention a direction of change in biodiversity component, and if so, what is the direction of the change?
Such as:
    "negative": biodiversity decline, reduction, degradation, fragmentation, homogenization, local loss, collapse risk, deterioration, or similar negative change is reported
    "positive": biodiversity increase, recovery, improvement, restoration, recolonization, or similar positive change is reported
    "mixed": both negative and positive biodiversity changes are reported in the text, whether across taxa, locations, times, or biodiversity measures
    "unclear": biodiversity change is mentioned, but the direction cannot be confidently determined from the text
    "none": no biodiversity change is reported or implied in the input text

## Step 3. Direct Anthropogenic driver or stressor
Does the input text mention or plausibly imply one or more direct anthropogenic driver(s) or stressor(s)?
These can be broader direct drivers of biodiversity loss or specific human activity sources such as defined by IUCN Threat classifications. Infer human activity stressors of biodiversity change only when input text supports it.

## Step 4. Linkage
Does the input text report or plausibly imply a link between direct anthropogenic driver(s) and biodiversity change(s), and if so, what is the basis of that link?

A weak but plausible text-grounded linkage may still count as 1 if the text clearly frames the human pressure as threatening, causing, or being associated with the biodiversity deterioration.
